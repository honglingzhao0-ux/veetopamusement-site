# FEC vs Mall Arcade: Comparing Two Business Models

Where you put your machines is not a location decision. It is a decision about what company you are building.

A standalone family entertainment centre and an arcade unit inside a shopping mall look similar from the outside. But they are two different businesses. One is a property business with entertainment attached. The other is a retail unit inside someone else's building, where visibility and rent depend on a landlord.

For which cabinets go in each venue type, see our guide on [mall atrium versus standalone arcade machine mix](/blog/mall-atrium-vs-standalone-arcade-machine-mix/). Here we look at capital, rent, people, risk and exit.

## The two businesses in one paragraph

**A standalone FEC is heavy-asset, long-cycle and repeat-visit.** You sign a long lease, build a room, install a full machine mix, staff it, and wait out a ramp-up period while the local market learns you exist. Once it works, the customer comes to you: families return weekly because the venue is part of their routine. You carry the property, staffing and equipment risk yourself.

**A mall arcade unit is lighter-asset, landlord-dependent and location-dependent.** You take a smaller footprint inside a building that already produces thousands of people a day and trade on the visibility of your shopfront. If the landlord changes the tenancy mix, your revenue can halve without anyone doing anything wrong.

## Side-by-side comparison of the business models

| Dimension | Standalone FEC | Mall arcade unit |
|---|---|---|
| Startup capital | Heavy - fitout, machinery, deposit, working capital | Lighter - fitout and machines; landlord often funds works |
| Lease length | Typically 5-10 years | Typically 3-5 years with renewal option |
| Rent structure | Fixed monthly rent, sometimes a turnover clause above a threshold | Fixed rent per m², base plus turnover share, or pure turnover rent; landlord often funds fitout |
| Staffing | 4-8 people: manager, attendants, technicians, prize counter | 2-3 people; landlord usually supplies cleaning and security |
| Revenue per m² | Lower per m², but the space is entirely yours | Higher per m², because the footfall is the landlord's asset |
| Customer profile and frequency | Families and groups who plan the visit, returning weekly | Families, teenagers and passers-by, mostly unplanned, many try once |
| Acquisition cost | Low - local marketing, brand building, word of mouth | Low if you inherit mall traffic, high if you must buy it |
| Competitive environment | You define it, usually the only FEC in the catchment | One of many, often alongside branded operators |
| Cash flow shape | Negative for 3-6 months, then seasonal | Negative for 1-3 months, then monthly landlord reconciliation |
| Exit difficulty | Medium - machines are portable, the lease is the constraint | High - you are bound to the landlord's calendar |
| Expansion path | Brand, second site, franchise model | More units in the same mall group, then regional rollout |
| Experience needed | High - you run a property and a business | Lower - the building does part of the work |

## Capital and risk, side by side

The figures below are **typical industry estimate ranges only**. They vary with city, unit size, the shell condition and how much work the landlord carries. Treat them as a budgeting framework, not a price list.

| Cost line | Standalone FEC (300-600 m²) | Mall unit (60-120 m²) |
|---|---|---|
| Fitout and building services | 60,000 - 200,000 USD | 15,000 - 50,000 USD |
| Machine package and freight | 70,000 - 160,000 USD | 30,000 - 70,000 USD |
| Opening prize and consumable stock | 5,000 - 12,000 USD | 3,000 - 8,000 USD |
| Signage, branding and launch marketing | 5,000 - 20,000 USD | 5,000 - 20,000 USD |
| Deposit, advance rent, permits | 15,000 - 50,000 USD | 10,000 - 40,000 USD |
| Working capital reserve | 20,000 - 60,000 USD | 10,000 - 25,000 USD |
| **Total funding requirement** | **175,000 - 500,000+ USD** | **70,000 - 190,000 USD** |
| Typical payback on total spend | 24-48 months | 12-24 months |
| Main failure mode | Ramp-up slower than planned | Landlord reshuffles the tenancy mix |

Payback on a mall unit is usually faster, but less controllable. A standalone FEC that works becomes an annuity: a loyal customer base and predictable weekly patterns. A mall unit that works is still a tenancy, and the landlord can turn your unit into a phone accessory shop in 36 months. And its higher revenue per square metre is partly borrowed - the footfall came from an anchor tenant paying rent on 4,000 m². If that anchor leaves, your revenue per m² falls with it.

## The minimum viable area for a standalone FEC

Area decides whether a standalone venue can carry its overheads, and the requirement follows from real machine footprints rather than a rule of thumb.

| Machine type | Example model | Catalogue footprint | Realistic occupied area with clearance and service access |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full-size claw crane | W78 x D85 x H190 cm | 0.66 m² | 3.5 - 4.5 m² |
| Four-player claw crane | W134 x D134 x H182 cm | 1.80 m² | 5 - 6 m² |
| Key and prize machine | W80 x D80 x H220 cm | 0.64 m² | 2.5 - 3.5 m² |
| Mini claw crane | W47 x D55 x H100 cm | 0.26 m² | 1.5 - 2 m² |
| Motion simulator | W230 x D280 x H250 cm | 6.44 m² | 10 - 12 m² |
| Two-player sports game | W225 x D130 x H98 cm | 2.93 m² | 5 - 6 m² |
| Six-player carousel | W230 x D230 x H250 cm | 5.29 m² | 9 - 11 m² |

Read the last column, not the third. A simulator occupying 6.4 m² needs 10 to 12 m² once you allow for the player in front, the queue behind, and a service route to the coin box. Multiply every machine's real area by 1.3 to 1.4 for aisles, corners and the prize counter, and you get the number that must fit the lease.

**The practical conclusion:** a standalone FEC needs roughly 250 m² of continuous floor for a full earning mix of simulators, redemption games, cranes and kiddie rides. Below about 150 m² you cannot fit enough revenue machines to cover a staff rota plus rent and utilities. Below 80 m² the arithmetic does not work.

## The mall unit variables that decide the outcome

For a mall unit, the machine list is the least of it. These variables decide whether the unit works, and all are settled before you order a cabinet.

- **Footfall at your frontage, not mall-wide footfall.** Count people passing your door between 12:00 and 20:00 on a weekday and a weekend. Mall totals count the whole building.
- **Visibility.** A unit at the end of a corridor with a blank wall opposite is not seen from the main flow. Ask for the sightline, or use a lit-up claw bank as a beacon - which is why [claw crane placement](/blog/claw-crane-prize-cost-and-margin-breakdown/) matters more here than anywhere else.
- **Rent structure.** Compare fixed rent, a base-plus-turnover split and pure turnover rent against your expected revenue. Percentage rent looks flexible, but at low revenue it leaves you carrying fixed costs without relief. Fixed rent gives you operating leverage and punishes you when you succeed.
- **Rent-free fit-out period.** A negotiation for 4 to 8 weeks rent-free plus 1 to 3 months of relief is a normal objective and often achievable. It is the highest-value item in the lease.
- **Who fits the shopfront.** Some landlords require a fascia style or signage package you pay for. Get it in writing before you sign.
- **Exclusivity.** Ask whether you can hold the category. A landlord signing two arcade units facing each other has removed the reason people walk in.

## The staged route, and why it works for most first-time operators

For someone opening their first venue, the sequence that carries the least risk is: **mall unit first, standalone FEC second.**

### Why this sequence is the safer bet

1. **A mall unit is a paid education.** In six months you learn what your local families spend, which games they play twice, and your real revenue per machine per day. You will get this wrong for the first month whatever you do, and a shorter lease makes that cheaper.
2. **Your downside is capped** at the lease, machines and deposit.
3. **You end up with data, and data gets you the next lease.** Six months of play logs lets you negotiate a standalone site on proven numbers, not projections.
4. **Equipment migrates.** Many machines from a mall unit are reusable in a standalone venue.

What the mall unit cannot give you is brand, control of the customer journey, or the ability to build a loyalty base. It is a stepping stone, not a destination.

## When you should not open a standalone FEC

Most failed amusement venues are not machine failures. They are capital and lease failures.

- **You do not have 6 to 9 months of fixed costs in reserve** beyond the build budget. If payroll and rent start before revenue does, you negotiate from a position of no leverage.
- **You have never run a venue with staff.** A standalone FEC is a shift business: opening, closing, cash handling, machine faults, a broken crane at 18:00 on a Saturday. If you are buying the equipment because you like machines, that is a hobby, not a plan.
- **You do not have a site with at least five years of secure tenure**, ideally with an option to renew at a pre-agreed rent.
- **The catchment does not support it.** A site needs families within a ten-minute drive, plus something nearby that creates dwell time - a cinema, food court or sports centre.
- **The building needs too much work.** If the shell needs structural changes, a new power supply or a new entrance, the fitout figure above stops being an estimate.

## Ten questions to decide which model fits you

Answer honestly and count your yeses.

1. Can you fund 6 to 9 months of fixed operating costs from reserves, separate from the build budget?
2. Can you commit to a lease of five years or longer without a penalty clause?
3. Have you managed a venue with employees for at least a year?
4. Is the standalone site continuous floor of 250 m² or more in one piece?
5. Is there an anchor nearby - cinema, food court, sports centre - that generates dwell traffic without you?
6. Are families within a ten-minute drive of the site?
7. Can you survive a lease negotiation where the landlord holds all the leverage?
8. Do you have someone who can cover the business for a month while you are ill?
9. Will you accept a slow start, or do you need revenue in the first three months?
10. Can you commit 12 to 18 months of your own time, unpaid, before it pays back?

**Six or more yeses** generally means a standalone FEC is a reasonable target. **Three or fewer** means start with a mall unit and build the operating record first.
